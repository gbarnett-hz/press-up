# The Angle Menu

Research angles are the slots the Researchers fill. Assign one angle per researcher, four to five per commission, and always include the adversarial angle. Each angle below has a one-line mandate and the questions it must answer.

## 1. Evidence & data
**Mandate:** the empirical record.
- What do the numbers actually say? Studies, datasets, statistics, measurements.
- Which figures are solid, which are contested, and where do the estimates disagree?
- What do the data NOT show - what claims float around without empirical backing?

## 2. History & context
**Mandate:** how we got here.
- The timeline: what happened, in what order, and what came before that matters.
- The background a reader needs to understand the present.
- The turning points - where decisions or events could have gone differently.

## 3. Voices & players
**Mandate:** the people in the story.
- Who are the key actors: experts, institutions, companies, governments, affected people?
- What do they say on the record? Direct quotes, positions, incentives, conflicts of interest.
- Who is noticeably silent, and why might that be?

## 4. Adversarial view (mandatory - never omitted)
**Mandate:** the strongest case against the expected thesis.
- Steelman the opposition: the best version of the argument against the piece's likely position.
- Where is the mainstream or popular account weakest - evidence it ignores, nuance it flattens?
- Who are the most credible critics of the central claims, and what exactly do they say?

## 5. Stakes & futures
**Mandate:** why the reader should care.
- The consequences: who wins, who loses, what breaks, what changes?
- Second-order effects - what happens because of what happens.
- The decision points ahead: what will people have to choose, and when?

## Substitution rules

- The Editor may substitute angles to fit the subject (e.g. a legal angle for a court story, a technical angle for an engineering story) but must keep four to five researchers and must keep the adversarial angle.
- The Journalist may split or merge the non-adversarial angles during Phase 1 if research demands it, and must say so in its planning note.
